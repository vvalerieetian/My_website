US brands were producing content for Chinese-speaking audiences that simply wasn't landing. I diagnosed why, rebuilt the strategy around genuine localization, and turned A/B testing into a paid media efficiency engine — while redesigning La Mer's creator collaboration workflow on the side.

The challenge

UCLA Health and San Francisco Travel both wanted to reach Chinese-speaking consumers — a high-value audience with distinct platform habits, content expectations, and cultural reference points. Their existing content was translated, not localized, and the performance data showed it.

What I did

1. Diagnosed before prescribing. I analyzed the performance data to pinpoint exactly where content was losing the audience — format, tone, platform choice, and cultural framing — rather than assuming "more content" was the answer.

2. Designed a localized content and paid media strategy. Content was rebuilt around what Chinese-speaking consumers actually engage with, not around translated versions of the English calendar.

3. Ran iterative A/B testing on paid media. Systematic creative and audience testing drove a 20% paid media efficiency gain and a 25% improvement in LinkedIn CTR.

4. Built the reporting infrastructure. I created operational reporting workflows and a performance tracking system (CTR, CPM, engagement) so the team could spot creative optimization levers continuously — not just at campaign wrap-up.

Ops highlight: redesigning La Mer's creator workflow

For La Mer's KFS campaign, I identified inefficiencies in how creators were matched with the advertiser and briefed. I redesigned the end-to-end creative collaboration process — from creator onboarding to asset delivery — improving production efficiency and brand-creator alignment.

I also managed cross-functional coordination across creative, strategy, and client stakeholders to deliver campaign materials on tight deadlines for Estée Lauder Corporate, bbq.chicken, JPAX, and Cyberbay.
